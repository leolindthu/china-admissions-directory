# Planning a China or Hong Kong university application while still in high school

**Prepared 9 October 2026 by Agora Scholars**, an independent admissions advisory business and the owner of this resource. This is a planning framework, not a university’s official timetable or a personal eligibility decision. Sources distinguish the entry year actually retrieved; older requirements are not promoted into a new cycle.

Start with three choices: the qualification you will finish, the subject you want to study, and the teaching language you can work in. Then check the exact university route. Preparing for a Chinese-taught degree at Peking University is different from preparing for an English-taught engineering route at Shanghai Jiao Tong or an international-qualifications application at HKU.

## Before you lock in school subjects

Build a small working shortlist. For each programme, record its official name, campus, entry year, teaching language and admissions route. Read the qualification and subject requirements before selecting A-levels, IB subjects or another school curriculum. Ask your school which subjects and predicted results it can support.

Keep academic preparation and evidence separate. Being interested in engineering is different from having the mathematics or science qualifications a particular route asks for. Equally, a list of example qualifications does not mean every applicant must take every listed examination. The [Tsinghua eligibility page](https://international.join-tsinghua.edu.cn/Admission1/Eligibility.htm) gives several kinds of academic evidence, while [CUHK's international requirements](https://admission.cuhk.edu.hk/application/overseas-other-qualifications-non-local-international-team/requirements/) distinguish general, language and programme-specific requirements.

Do not postpone the applicant-status check. Nationality, residence history and local/nonlocal classifications can affect which route applies. A foreign passport or an international school curriculum does not resolve every question. Use the university's instructions and relevant authorities for a determination; an adviser can help organise the questions and records.

## Build a language plan around the actual degree

Check entry requirements, the language used in teaching and assessment, and any language requirement for graduation. These are different questions. Plan test dates with room for results, possible retakes and the university's validity period; do not assume all institutions accept the same test formats or waivers.

## During the school years before application

Keep an organised private record of school reports, examination results and substantive work. For each activity or project, record what you actually did, the time involved and what you learned. The purpose is to understand and explain your experience, not to accumulate impressive labels or invent achievements. There is no universal activity checklist or guaranteed admissions formula.

Review the shortlist periodically as your interests and results develop. Compare course content and prerequisites, not just university names. For questions about clubs, daily life or career prospects, distinguish official institutional information from a named student's personal experience and from employment statistics with a defined cohort and date. A famous graduate is not evidence that every student has the same opportunities.

Plan costs without assuming a scholarship, job or admission outcome. Keep tuition, accommodation, living costs, travel, insurance and application-related expenses separate. Use current programme and scholarship sources when setting amounts; this framework supplies no universal budget.

## In the application year

Replace planning references with the current route notice. Make a checklist for requirements, the supporting record, who provides it and the official deadline. Ask the school early about predicted grades, transcripts, references and translations. Keep original records securely; an initial adviser enquiry does not need passport scans or full private application files.

Check deadlines and time zones individually. Scholarship, portfolio, interview, examination and admissions schedules may be separate. Minimum eligibility is not an offer or a probability of acceptance. You should be able to explain the facts and academic interests in your own application.

Two cautions from the sources reviewed deserve an explicit note:

- **Tsinghua:** the FAQ's statement that CSCA is not required expressly names the **2026 intake**, while other answers refer to 2027 and some age/guardianship dates are older. Do not treat that answer as a confirmed 2027 exemption. Consult the [current eligibility page](https://international.join-tsinghua.edu.cn/Admission1/Eligibility.htm) and your programme's instructions.
- **HKU:** the [2027 general-information document](https://admissions.hku.hk/sites/default/files/2026-09/HKU_General_Information_NJ_2027.pdf) and [2027 application guide](https://admissions.hku.hk/sites/default/files/2026-09/UG_NJ_Admissions%20StepbyStep%20Guide_2027Intake.pdf) differ about changing programme choices after submission. Finalise choices before submitting; ask HKU directly if a change is necessary. This guide does not promise that an edit will be accepted.

## After an offer: keep immigration and admissions separate

Admission does not itself grant permission to enter or study. Hong Kong has its own [student visa/entry-permit process](https://www.immd.gov.hk/eng/services/visas/study.html); a mainland-only visa checklist is not suitable for every HKU or CUHK applicant. Requirements depend on the person's status and circumstances. Work with the admitting institution and the responsible immigration authority, including any arrangements for applicants under 18.

An exchange semester, language programme and full degree can follow different application, credit and immigration processes. This undergraduate planning guide does not verify exchange nominations, credit transfer, residence formalities or employment permission. Those topics need separate source-grounded guides.
